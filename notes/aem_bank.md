# CEM and AEM are not the same stack

CEM is the concentrator between the leach pump and EW-1. The liquor is still below pH 1.5. Cations cross the cation film. The concentrate, about 0.17 m³/h at 20 g/L, goes to EW-1. Diluate and the high-differential-pressure bypass return to CSTR 1. This stack does not see borate, phosphate, or fluoride as a cut.

AEM is later. Spent brine after the polish bank is raised to pH 9, and digest liquor joins it before the silica drop. At that pH the anions are B(OH)4−, HPO4 2−, SO4 2−, and F−. They cross the anion film into one concentrate. The stack does not split them. Diluate is sodium-potassium brine and is the feed to the potassium crystallizer.

The crystallizer is 5–10 °C. KCl goes to the farm. NaCl mother liquor goes to BMED. The nitrogen scrub is not on this liquor. It is a packed tower on burnout gas and pyrolyzer gas. Hydrochloric acid absorbs ammonia. Ammonium chloride goes to the farm.


The anion stack does not float. Two liquors enter it. Post-polish brine, after the polish bank, is raised to pH 9. Digest liquor joins it before the silica drop. Arsenic does not enter. Diluate is the sodium-potassium brine and is the feed to the potassium crystallizer.

K split is a polypropylene crystallizer at 5–10 °C on that diluate. Potassium chloride crystallizes and goes to the farm. The mother liquor is sodium chloride and goes to the BMED salt chamber.

N scrub is not on the liquor. It is a packed tower on burnout off-gas and pyrolyzer non-condensables, after the demister. BMED hydrochloric acid absorbs ammonia from nylon, resin, and biomass. Ammonium chloride goes to the farm. Nitrogen oxides stay on the alkaline scrubber.

CRU is the chlorate unit, not a BMED chamber. Chlorine from the electrowinning anodes and from the three chloride pots is absorbed in sodium hydroxide. The product is sodium chlorate, returned with BMED hydrochloric acid through regen leach to CSTR 1. It does not make the acid or the base.

The stack moves anions. It does not fractionate them. Borate, phosphate, sulfate, and fluoride all pass an anion film at pH 9. The split is on the concentrate, after the stack.

Post-polish brine is acidic. A polypropylene tank raises it to pH 9 with BMED sodium hydroxide so borate is B(OH)4− and phosphate is HPO4 2−. Digest liquor is already alkaline and is cut before the silica drop. Arsenic does not enter. At this pH arsenite is mostly neutral, so a missed arsenic cut would not report cleanly to the concentrate. PK-10 is the arsenic takeoff.

Stack: polypropylene frame, anion film, 16 pairs, 0.25 m², 4 m² installed. Electrode rinse is acidic. Anode is titanium with a lead-dioxide coat. Clean-in-place is BMED hydrochloric acid only. Diluate is the sodium-rich brine and goes to the potassium crystallizer, then to the BMED salt feed.

Concentrate cuts, in order:

- Fluoride. A slip of calcium hydroxide drops calcium fluoride. That cake feeds the HF electrodialysis cell. It does not go to the farm.
- Sulfate. Cool to drop sodium sulfate. That salt is the PK-5B double-sulfate reagent. Excess returns to BMED.
- Phosphate. What remains after those two drops goes to the farm.
- Borate. Stays dissolved through the sulfate drop. Acidify with BMED hydrochloric acid and calcine to boron oxide for the boron retort.

No carbon bed on this bank. Chlorate attacks it.
