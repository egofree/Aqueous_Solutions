# Carbide train and electrochemical fluorination

Document number: AQS-NOTE-CARBIDE  
Revision: A  
Date: 2026-10-16  
Authority: response to route questions. Not a PE stamp.

## Why direct electrochemical fluorination was rejected

Electrochemical fluorination (Simons cell or similar) fluorinates organics in anhydrous HF at a nickel anode. For VDF and TFE it is a poor fit:

- Selectivity to the exact unsaturated monomers is low; the cell produces a mixture of perfluorocarbons and partially fluorinated species that still require separation and further reaction.
- Energy demand is high (fluorine equivalent is generated electrochemically rather than taken from already-recovered HF + Cl₂).
- It requires anhydrous HF handling and a new equipment class not present elsewhere in the plant.
- The methanol → chloromethane → HF route is industrially proven for both R-22/TFE and VDF precursors, closes on feeds the plant already has (methanol, CRU chlorine, HF_GEN), and returns HCl to the existing acid loop.

ECF remains a documented alternate only if a future requirement appears for a perfluorinated species that the chloromethane route cannot make. It is not the baseline.

## Should the plant have a carbide train?

Calcium carbide (CaC₂) from CaO + 3 C at ~2000 °C, then CaC₂ + 2 H₂O → C₂H₂ + Ca(OH)₂, supplies acetylene. Acetylene is a versatile C₂ feedstock.

Arguments for adding it:

- The plant already produces calcium metal and CaO-equivalent hydroxides, and has pyro char. The raw materials exist.
- Acetylene enables routes to vinyl chloride, acetaldehyde, and some fine chemicals that methanol alone does not reach as directly.
- A few monomer or solvent paths become shorter.

Arguments against making it baseline now:

- The locked VDF/TFE route does not need it.
- The high-temperature carbide furnace is another 2000 °C class unit (similar duty to the silicon arc) with its own containment and CO handling.
- Methanol, CO, and pyro char already cover the carbon needs of the routes that are locked (monomers, silicon, chlorination).
- Adding it before a specific process requires acetylene creates an unused high-temperature train.

**Decision.** Do not add a carbide train as a baseline unit. Record it as an available future option if a locked route later requires acetylene. The calcium and carbon feeds are already in the plant, so the train can be added without new external materials when a duty appears. No graph node is added in this revision.
