## 1. Purpose and scope

A header is a common pipe or duct that collects a service from its sources and distributes it to its users. This page sizes the 8 header units on the plant graph: their bore, length, internal volume and standing holdup. The class, schedule and rating of each line connected to a header are given in AQS-PIP-003 and AQS-PIP-004, not here.

## 2. Method

The bore of each header is the diameter that carries the concurrent design flow at the velocity limit of its service, d = sqrt(4Q/(πv)) ([PERRY9] section 6). The limits are 8.0 m/s for gas, 1.5 m/s for water and 8.0 m/s for flue gas. The bore is not taken below the minimum size of the service, which is DN25 for gas, DN25 for water and DN100 for flue gas; these minimums allow for the seating of take-offs, instrument tappings, flue draught and condensate drainage.
The length of each header is the longest routed run of its lines in the route table of the isometrics AQS-ISO-002. Its volume and standing holdup follow from the bore and the length.
The flow in the hot-water bus `Q4_BUS` follows AQS-HEAT-LADDER-001. The winter concurrent case, which governs, is the sum of the life support heating, the HX-9 and HX-10 preheaters, the recharge of the ground heat exchanger and the make-up air coil, carried on a loop temperature swing of 10.0 K (driver `heat_loop_dT_K`). The summer case, in which the adsorption chiller serves the make-up air, is lower and does not govern.
