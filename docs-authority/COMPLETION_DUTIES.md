# Completion duties

AQS-DUTY-007. Plant rate is the assumptions-sheet point: leach discharge 2 m³/h at 2 g/L dissolved metal. Concentrate to EW-1 is 0.17 m³/h at 20 g/L. Energy may be external. Mass in is waste or natural feedstock only.

These units are parallel where the feeds differ. They are not a second electrowinning aisle.

## CONC-ED

CONC-ED sits between the leach pump and EW-1. It is not an evaporator.

The train is a CPVC coalescer, an alumina crossflow, then a cation-exchange stack. The coalescer takes free oil. No activated carbon: chlorate attacks it. The crossflow is 0.2 µm alumina, 3 m/s, 25 m², 80 L/m²·h. Cake backflushes to sink dewater. The stack is 20 pairs, 0.25 m² each, 5 m² installed. Anode is titanium with a lead-dioxide coat. Liquor is held under pH 1.5 so iron stays dissolved. Clean-in-place is hydrochloric acid from BMED only. Caustic drops iron in the housing. A high differential-pressure bypass returns to LEACH CSTR 1. Diluate returns to LEACH CSTR 1. The concentrate carries 85 % of the cations, 3.4 kg/h dissolved metal, into EW-1.

## Solids line

The solids line takes undissolved sink only. Dense plastic leaves at the vibratory split and does not enter burnout.

Order: countercurrent rinse, indirect burnout at 450 °C to 550 °C, dry mill under 300 µm, digest at 80 °C to 90 °C in BMED sodium hydroxide, alkali fusion of what the digest does not open. Soda-lime glass opens in the digest. Quartz, feldspar, and olivine do not. They recycle through a sodium hydroxide or sodium carbonate sinter at 300 °C to 500 °C and return to the digest. Grit that survives both steps is alumina, silicon carbide, and zircon. Rinse liquor returns to regen leach. Bromide condensate from the burnout goes to BMED.

## AEM bank

Two feeds. Post-polish brine is acidic. It is raised to pH 9 with BMED sodium hydroxide so borate moves as B(OH)₄⁻ and phosphate as HPO₄²⁻. Digest liquor is already alkaline and is cut before the silica drop. The stack is a polypropylene frame with an anion-exchange film. Diluate goes to the BMED salt feed. Borate concentrate is acidified and calcined to boron oxide for the boron retort. Phosphate goes to the farm. Sulfate goes to the PK-5B double-sulfate cut; excess goes to BMED. Fluoride goes to the HF electrodialysis cell, not the farm. Arsenic does not enter this stack.

## K split

Polypropylene cooled crystallizer, 5 °C to 10 °C, on the sodium–potassium brine after the anion bank and before BMED. Potassium chloride crystallizes and goes to the farm. The mother liquor is sodium chloride and goes to the BMED salt chamber.

## N scrub

Packed tower on burnout off-gas and pyrolyzer non-condensables, after the demister. BMED hydrochloric acid absorbs ammonia from nylon, resin, and biomass. Product is ammonium chloride to the farm. Nitrogen oxides are not this tower. They stay on the alkaline scrubber. There is no Haber loop on this sheet.

## PK-5B

Batch polypropylene lot tank. Not a flow cell. The PK-5 oxalate cake is dissolved in BMED hydrochloric acid. Sodium sulfate from the anion bank precipitates the lanthanum–cerium double sulfate. Neodymium/praseodymium and dysprosium/terbium stay in the liquor, are reprecipitated as oxalates at stepped pH, and are calcined to oxide lots. The lots go to the calciothermic retort. Oxalic acid is a consumable until a synthesis row exists.

## Molten-salt cells

Four duties. Three are electrolytic pots. One is a retort. They do not feed each other in series. The shared return is chlorine to the chlorate unit. No aqueous acid enters a pot. Aqueous cells do not make aluminum, magnesium, calcium, or the rare earths.

Aluminum. The hydroxide cake is calcined to alumina. Alumina is chlorinated with pyro char and recycled chlorine at about 800 °C to aluminum chloride. The chloride is electrolyzed in an aluminum chloride–sodium chloride melt near 700 °C. Graphite anode. Graphite or steel cathode. Chlorine returns to the chlorate unit and to the chlorinator.

Magnesium. The hydroxide is converted to chloride and dehydrated to anhydrous magnesium chloride under hydrogen chloride, so it does not hydrolyze to oxide. The anhydrous salt is electrolyzed in a magnesium chloride–sodium chloride melt near 700 °C. Steel cathode. Graphite anode. Chlorine returns to the chlorate unit.

Calcium. Calcium chloride is electrolyzed near 800 °C on a contact cathode. Calcium dissolves in its own chloride if it is left in the melt, so the metal is withdrawn as it forms. Part of the calcium is product. Part is the reductant for the rare-earth lots.

Rare earths. This is not an electrolytic pot. Oxide lots from PK-5B are reduced with calcium metal in a sealed steel retort at 900 °C to 1100 °C. Slag is calcium oxide and returns to the calcium-pot feed. Chlorine is not made in the retort.

## Silicon and boron

Two vessels. Boron oxide does not enter the silicon arc. It reports to the slag and contaminates the silicon.

Silicon. Washed, calcined silica gel plus pyro char in a submerged arc at about 1800 °C. The reaction is SiO₂ + 2C → Si + 2CO. Carbon monoxide goes to the gas header. Slag returns to alkali fusion. Metallurgical silicon is then directionally solidified to drop iron, aluminum, and calcium. That step does not refine boron.

Boron. Boron oxide from the anion bank is reduced in a retort with magnesium or calcium at about 850 °C. The oxide slag is leached with BMED hydrochloric acid. Boron powder is the product. The leached magnesium or calcium salt returns to the matching pot feed.
