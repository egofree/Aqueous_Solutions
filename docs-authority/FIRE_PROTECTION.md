## Fire water tank and fire pump (NFPA22_2023 / NFPA20_2025, drivers fire_* keys)

1. Tank (gravity supply): gravity: water-tower dead storage (primary) + CATCH_TANK suction source for the pump (second supply). Primary: 451 m3 fire reserve held as dead storage in the bottom of the water tower, below the hydro outlet nozzles, so a hydro discharge can never draw it; static head at L0 16.8 m (165 kPa), at the top life floor 10.8 m. Refill of the reserve within 8 h (`fire_tank_refill_h`, registered).
2. Pump (boosted supply): electric horizontal split-case, listed, NFPA 20; critical bus with the genset as second supply. Rated 5678 L/min (listed step over the governing 5013 L/min incl. hose allowance) at 150 kPa (static shortfall + `fire_pump_head_margin_kPa`); churn <= 182 kPa (1.21 x rated, NFPA 20 4.16); runout 8517 L/min at >= 98 kPa (NFPA 20 4.18, 150 % / 65 %); motor about 2.1 kW at the gravity-assisted head, jockey pump and weekly churn per NFPA 25.
Two independent supplies per the redundancy rule: the tower gravity reserve and the CATCH_TANK suction source for the pump (the lower reservoir also takes the sprinkler run-off), so a fire after a hydro discharge still has a full supply in one of the two reservoirs (AQS-BLD-014).

## Water supplies (two independent sources, redundancy rule)

1. Gravity: 451 m3 fire reserve held as dead storage in the bottom of the water tower, below the hydro outlet nozzles (NFPA22_2023), so a hydro discharge can never draw it. Static head at L0 16.8 m (165 kPa), at the top life floor 10.8 m. The reserve is split across the cells; each cell has its own fire outlet, so one cell drained for inspection leaves the rest.
2. Pumped: the NFPA20_2025 fire pump above draws from CATCH_TANK (lower reservoir, 3366 m3 when full; it also takes the sprinkler run-off) and is fed from the critical bus with the genset as its second supply. It boosts the gravity supply where hydraulics need more than the static head.
The lower reservoir is sized to hold the hydro volume plus the fire reserve (AQS-BLD-014), so a fire after a hydro discharge still has a full supply in one of the two reservoirs.

## Gas-receiver fire case (API521_2020, carried from X-SAF-04)

Gas-filled tubes: A = F' A'/sqrt(P1), F' = 0.1406/(C Kd) (Tw - T1)^1.25 / T1^0.6625 (US units), Tw 1100 F (593 C), P1 at 121 % of set (fire overpressure). Liquid CO2: Q = 21,000 F A^0.82 Btu/h on the wetted tube area (drainage and fire-fighting present, F 1), relieved as vapour.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRYO_H2 | 32 | 70.0 | 26721 | 344 | 116.1 | 12.2 | 0.21 | 13 | 8.6 | 1.41 |
| CRYO_O2 | 16 | 35.0 | 26721 | 344 | 58.2 | 8.6 | 0.28 | 9 | 68.8 | 1.92 |
| CRYO_CO2 | 4 | 8.8 | 2763 | 283 | 61.1 | 8.8 | 0.28 | 9 | 0.0 | 0.00 |

The fire case governs every receiver: the earlier 3.3 mm bore covered only the blocked outlet. Each manifolded half now has its own relief valve sized for the fire on its tubes (bore in the table, propagated to the graph relief_mm and the relief register). For gas-filled tubes API 521 warns that a relief valve alone may not prevent wall rupture in a fire, because the unwetted wall heats and loses strength while the pressure stays near set. The H2 and O2 banks therefore get automatic depressuring on flame detection to 50 % of design gauge in 15 min (API 521 5.20, wall 13.5 mm), sized in the table at twice the average rate for the initial peak, plus NFPA 15 water spray to keep the tubes cool. The depressuring vents go to the same safe-location vent stacks as the relief valves.
