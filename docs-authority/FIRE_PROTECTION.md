## 1. Purpose and scope

This page states the fire protection of the plant: the sprinkler demand of each level and hazard zone, the sprinkler piping, the two fire-water supplies, the fire detection, and the fire case of the high-pressure gas receivers. It is a study. The fire engineer of record confirms the hazard classes and the hydraulic calculation.
The hazard classes are the occupancy classes of NFPA 13 [1]: light hazard (LH), ordinary hazard group 1 and group 2 (OH1, OH2) and extra hazard group 1 (EH1). The lower flammability limit is abbreviated LFL.

## 4. Sprinkler piping

The sprinkler pipe is Schedule 40 black steel to ASTM A53. The system is hydraulically designed by the density and area method. The pipe schedule method of NFPA 13 chapter 7 [1] is not used, because it does not cover extra hazard occupancies and level L0 contains EH1 zones.
The riser and feed mains carry the design demand of 5013 L/min at a velocity of 6 m/s. This needs a bore of 133 mm, so the riser is DN150. Each branch main is sized for its zone demand at the same velocity.
The fire engineer of record makes the hydraulic calculation from the riser to the hydraulically most remote area. The fire pump head includes a design margin of 150 kPa (driver `fire_pump_head_margin_kPa`) to cover it.

## 5. Water supplies

The plant has two independent fire-water supplies, as the redundancy rule requires. Each can deliver the design demand, so a fire after a hydro discharge, or with one supply out of service, still has a full supply.

### 5.1 Gravity supply

The gravity supply is the primary supply. The fire reserve is 451 m³, held as dead storage at the bottom of the water tower below the hydro outlet nozzles, so a hydro discharge cannot draw it [2]. The reserve is split across the tower cells and each cell has its own fire outlet, so one cell drained for inspection leaves the others in service.
The static head is 16.8 m (165 kPa) at level L0 and 10.8 m at the top floor of the habitation. The reserve is refilled within 8 h (driver `fire_tank_refill_h`).

### 5.2 Pumped supply

The fire pump is a listed electric horizontal split-case pump to NFPA 20 [3]. It is fed from the critical bus, with the backup generator as its second supply. It draws from the lower reservoir `CATCH_TANK`, which holds 3366 m³ when full and also collects the sprinkler run-off. The lower reservoir is sized for the hydro volume plus the fire reserve (AQS-BLD-014). The pump boosts the gravity supply where the hydraulics need more than the static head.
The pump is rated 5678 L/min, the next listed size above the design demand of 5013 L/min, at 150 kPa. That head is the shortfall of the static head plus the design margin. The shut-off pressure is at most 182 kPa (1.21 times rated, NFPA 20 4.16). At 150 % of rated flow, 8517 L/min, the pump delivers at least 98 kPa, which is 65 % of rated head (NFPA 20 4.18). The motor draws about 2.1 kW at this head because the gravity supply provides most of it. A jockey pump holds the system pressure, and the pump is run weekly at shut-off as NFPA 25 [4] requires.

## 7. Fire case of the gas receivers

The relief valves of the gas receivers are sized for a fire under API 521 [9]. For tubes filled with gas the required area is A = F' A'/√P1, with F' = 0.1406/(C Kd) (Tw − T1)^1.25 / T1^0.6625 in US customary units, a wall temperature Tw of 1100 °F (593 °C) and P1 at 121 % of the set pressure. For the liquid carbon dioxide the heat input is Q = 21,000 F A^0.82 Btu/h on the wetted tube area, with F = 1 because drainage and fire-fighting are present; the liquid is relieved as vapour. Table 7.1 gives the results. The blocked-outlet bore is the relief case without fire, for comparison.

Table 7.1. Fire case of the gas receivers

| Receiver | Tubes | Exposed area (m²) | P1 (kPa abs) | T1 (K) | Fire area per half bank (mm²) | Fire bore per half bank (mm) | Blocked-outlet bore (mm) | Installed bore, two valves (mm) | Depressuring release (kg) | Depressuring bore (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRYO_H2 | 32 | 70.0 | 26721 | 344 | 116.1 | 12.2 | 0.21 | 13 | 8.6 | 1.41 |
| CRYO_O2 | 16 | 35.0 | 26721 | 344 | 58.2 | 8.6 | 0.28 | 9 | 68.8 | 1.92 |
| CRYO_CO2 | 4 | 8.8 | 2763 | 283 | 61.1 | 8.8 | 0.28 | 9 | 0.0 | 0.00 |

The fire case governs every receiver. Each manifolded half bank has its own relief valve sized for a fire on its tubes, and the installed bore is written to the graph field `relief_mm` and to the relief register.
For tubes filled with gas, API 521 [9] warns that a relief valve alone may not prevent rupture in a fire, because the wall that no liquid wets heats up and loses strength while the pressure stays near the set pressure. The hydrogen and oxygen banks therefore depressure automatically on flame detection to 50 % of the design gauge pressure within 15 min (API 521 5.20, for a wall of 13.5 mm). The depressuring bore in Table 7.1 is sized at twice the average rate to cover the initial peak. Water spray keeps the tubes cool [8]. The depressuring vents discharge to the same vent stacks as the relief valves.
