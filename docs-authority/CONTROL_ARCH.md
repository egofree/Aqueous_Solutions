## 1. Purpose and scope

The plant is run by programmable logic controllers (PLCs), which read the instruments and drive the pumps, valves and other actuators. This page states the control philosophy: how the controllers are divided, which of them are duplicated, how safety and fire functions are kept apart from basic control, and how the controllers are networked and programmed. It then counts the input and output (I/O) points of each controller and sizes its I/O modules and its 24 V supply.

## 2. Control philosophy

- Each sector has its own PLC, so that a fault in one sector stays in that sector. The plant runs from the set-points in the flow graph. Every control loop is a single-input, single-output proportional-integral (PI) loop unless the design of the unit calls for a cascade.
- A sector whose controller, if lost, would put the occupants, their air, water or food, or the plant power at risk before it could be repaired has a redundant pair of central processing units (CPUs). The pair switches over without a bump in the outputs, and it has two 24 V supplies fed from the critical bus, which is backed by a stored-energy supply to NFPA 111. These sectors and the reasons are listed in Table 3.1. Every other sector has a single CPU.
- Safety trips are not part of the basic control PLCs. They are handled by the safety functions of task X-CTL-03, which follow the hazard and operability study (HAZOP) of task X-SAF-01. The fire alarm is a separate panel to NFPA72_2025; it sends signals to the PLCs and never receives them.
- All PLCs, the operator screens (HMIs) and the data historian are on one fibre-optic ring using the Media Redundancy Protocol (MRP) of IEC62439_2. A single cut in the ring is recovered within 200 ms. Brushless DC (BLDC) motor drivers and stepper drivers are on a fieldbus within each sector.
- The programs are written in IEC61131_3 structured text, kept in the repository, and simulated against the flow graph before they are loaded into the controllers (task X-CTL-04).

## 3. Redundant CPU pairs

Table 3.1 lists the sectors whose PLC has a redundant CPU pair.

Table 3.1. Sectors with a redundant CPU pair

| Sector | Why its controller is redundant |
|---|---|
| CRYO | oxygen and hydrogen storage and the habitat air handling |
| ELEC | plant power and the critical bus (PV, flow battery, genset and fuel cell are ELEC units) |
| FARM | food and the grow-bed air loop of the habitat |
| GAS | the hydrogen and oxygen headers and their relief |
| GEO | the ground-source heat pumps |
| HAB | the occupied habitat |
| NUT | nutrient dosing of the grow beds (FARM) |
| PUMPED_HYDRO | the backup energy store of the critical bus |
| WTR | drinking water and sanitation for the occupants |

The following sectors are kept on a single CPU by decision of the project owner:

- HEAT: Owner decision 2026-10-24 (AQS-OQ-001 Q-014): HEAT holds the insulated thermal stores (Q1 to Q4 tiers) and their heat exchangers. A controller outage stops heat moving between the stores; the stores keep their heat, losing only their standing loss. It is not a life-support or power function.
