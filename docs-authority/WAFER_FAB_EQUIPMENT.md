## 1. Purpose and scope

The plant makes its own silicon wafers from imported polysilicon. This list gives the fab units on the plant graph, the method chosen to grow the crystal, the tool train inside the fab unit, the streams of the fab with their rates, and the mass balance. The reagent synthesis forks are listed in AQS-EQP-FAB-RGN-001 and the solar-cell tools in AQS-EQP-FAB-PV-001.

## 2. Terms

- Czochralski pulling: growing a single-crystal ingot (boule) by slowly drawing a seed crystal from molten silicon.
- Fluidised-bed reactor: a reactor that grows polysilicon granules in a bed of particles kept suspended by gas.
- Lapping: flattening wafers between plates with an abrasive slurry.
- RCA clean: the standard two-step wet clean for silicon wafers.
- Average load: the continuous electrical load of a unit on the graph (`continuous_kw`).

Other abbreviations are in `docs-authority/GLOSSARY.md`.

## 4. Crystal-growth method

Two methods were compared for growing the crystal: Czochralski pulling, which melts the silicon at about 1414 °C and draws a single-crystal ingot from a seed, and a fluidised-bed reactor, which grows polysilicon granules from a powder feed. The first plant uses a Czochralski puller with an imported polysilicon charge. The fluidised-bed reactor is kept as an alternative for when the powder form or the number of fabs warrants it; a second crystal forge is not modelled.

### 4.1 Silicon charge and blanket gas

The fab starts 5 wafers a day of 27.8 g. Each wafer takes its own thickness and the saw kerf along the ingot, and the charge adds the crown, tail, diameter grind and pot scrap. A 7 d tool campaign needs 1.58 kg of charge, but a 40.5 cm crucible needs at least 33.5 kg of melt to pull from, so the charge is 33.5 kg. It yields about 740 wafers from a 61 cm body, enough for 148 d of starts; the wafers are stored until started. The silicon feed is 0.2264 kg/d on IA-SI-IMPORT and STOCK_TO_FAB. The wafers, 0.1389 kg/d, run through the lithography units and back to the fab before going to the dock; the remaining 0.0875 kg/d leaves as kerf and crop on IA-FAB-KERF.

The plant makes only metallurgical-grade silicon, in SI_REDUCE, which is far below the purity of a crystal charge. The charge is therefore imported through the dock into METAL_STORE, which holds the larger of one charge and one 90 d resupply interval, 33.5 kg. An in-house route that refines SI_REDUCE silicon to charge grade would be the backup of a different technology; it is a task in AQS-PLAN-007.

The puller runs under argon from the cold box CRYO_ASU for 19.2 h a pull: meltdown, neck and crown, the 10.2 h body pull, and cool-down. At 39 slpm over a crucible of 1288 cm2 this is 79.3 kg of argon a pull, 0.536 kg/d on IA-AR-CZ averaged over the days between pulls. The exhaust returns on IA-AR-CZ-BACK to the heated getter of the cold box, so the loop is closed. Nitrogen is not offered as an alternative for the puller, because at the melt temperature it forms silicon nitride on the melt and the crucible; it remains the purge gas of the wafer tools on S03.

## 5. Tool train inside the fab unit

The crystal is grown, cut and polished inside WAFER_FAB. The stages run in this order: charge preparation, Czochralski pulling, controlled cooling and optional anneal, cropping and diameter grinding, wire sawing, edge profiling, lapping and chemical-mechanical polishing, a clean before handoff, and the wet and dry tools of the fab. The stages are equipment within WAFER_FAB, not separate units on the graph. Table 5.1 gives the equipment loads that make up the average load of WAFER_FAB.

Table 5.1. Equipment of the fab unit

| Equipment | Average load, kW |
| --- | --- |
| CZ puller: charge heat-up + hot zone over 19.2 h, one pull every 148 d | 0.1969 |
| ingot cool/anneal station: N2 circulator duty share | 0.0200 |
| crop/grind/edge tools at 5 starts/d | 0.0400 |
| FAB_TOOL dry row parasitics (ox, RTA, metal, probe standby) | 0.1200 |
| N2 purge distribution (ISO 14644 Class 6 row) | 0.0300 |

The equipment loads sum to 0.4069 kW against an average load of 0.4069 kW on the graph, so they agree.
