## Purpose and scope

The plant does not discharge process gas to outdoors as a normal end state. This page states that rule, lists each former open discharge with the path that now closes it, and checks every closure against the plant graph each time the graph is regenerated. The chemistry of the scrubbers is in AQS-GAS-002 and their sizes in AQS-EQP-VENT-001.

## 1. Terms

- Normal path: the streams that carry the vent in normal operation.
- Contingency path: a relief route kept for upsets and life safety, which carries no flow in normal operation.
- Closure: a normal path that ends in a unit that reuses the gas or its absorbed product, not in the atmosphere.

## 2. The rule

Every former open discharge ends in one of two ways. Toxic, acid and oxidising gases (hydrogen chloride, chlorine, bromine, fume and mist) go to a dedicated scrubber whose liquor is regenerated into a loop already on the plant. Condensable and separable gases (hydrogen, carbon dioxide, boil-off and the non-condensable gas after oxidation) go to the cryogenic plant for condensation and separation. Relief routes for upsets and for the authority having jurisdiction may remain, but they are not the normal discharge. No process vent shares a duct or a return with the habitation or the farm.

## 5. Safety notes

1. No duct carries a process atmosphere through the fire barriers into the habitation or the farm.
2. The acid-gas and bromine scrubbers have their fans after the packing and keep their liquors apart (AQS-GAS-002).
3. The afterburner still destroys tar and non-condensable gas; capture of its flue is downstream of the oxidation.
4. The relief routes stay available for upsets and life safety and are labelled as contingency.
5. The maximum allowable quantities of AQS-HSE-001 still apply.
6. The methanol tank breathes through a conservation vent with a flame arrester (NFPA 30); spilled methanol drains to a bonded sump, never to a water tank.
