## 1. Purpose and scope

This page sets the duty of two steps that treat woody and fibrous farm waste: hydrochar-assisted composting of woody trim, and the electrochemical advanced oxidation process (EAOP) pretreatment that releases sugars for the digester. It gives the feed rates, the conditions taken from the literature and where each product goes. Equipment sizing is outside its scope.

## 2. Hydrochar-assisted composting

Woody trim, 3 kg/d, passes through a low-temperature hydrothermal step and is then composted. Adding hydrochar to an aerobic compost pile reduces lignocellulose by 64.36 mg per gram of feed; the authors attribute this to electron transfer from the oxygen-bearing pore surface of the hydrochar [1].

On this feed the reduction is 0.19 kg/d, which leaves as carbon dioxide to the CO2 header. The compost mass is otherwise unchanged.

## 3. Electrochemical pretreatment

The pretreatment replaces the earlier 80 °C caustic hold. Table 3.1 gives the conditions used. They are taken from the published summary of a thesis from RMIT University [2]; the full thesis was not available for review, so these values should be confirmed against it.

Table 3.1. Pretreatment conditions

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Temperature | 25 °C |
| Cell voltage | 2.0 V |
| Residence time | 30 min |
| Solids loading | 25 wt% |
| Reducing-sugar yield | 204.4 mg per gram of feed |

On the 8 kg/d charge the yield is 1.64 kg/d of sugar. The sugar goes to the digester and returns to the farm as digestate.

The solvent is 10 wt% 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate (an ionic liquid) in γ-valerolactone [3]. The plant does not make the ionic liquid; it is outside the self-copy set.

## 4. Mass balance

The plant mass balance still closes with these changes: 1037.6 kg/d enters and leaves the plant boundary.
